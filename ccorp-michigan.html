Before You Start

What is a C-Corp and is it right for you?

A C-Corporation is a business that legally exists as its own separate person. It can own property, sign contracts, sue and be sued, and pay its own taxes, completely apart from the people who own it. When you hear "corporation" without a letter in front of it, this is what people mean. Every publicly traded company in America is a C-Corp.

The "C" comes from Subchapter C of the tax code. It is not something you elect or apply for. If you file Articles of Incorporation in Michigan and do nothing else, you have a C-Corp by default. Filing IRS Form 2553 afterward is what converts it to an S-Corp.

Here is the part that decides this for most people. A C-Corp pays corporate income tax on its profit. Then, when it distributes what's left to the owners as dividends, the owners pay personal income tax on that same money again. Two layers of tax on one pile of profit. That is double taxation, and it is the reason the overwhelming majority of small businesses should not be a C-Corp.

✓ Pros of a C-Corp

  • The only structure serious investors and VCs will fund
  • Unlimited shareholders, including foreign owners and other companies
  • Multiple classes of stock — common, preferred, voting, non-voting
  • Strong liability protection separating you from the business
  • Can deduct the full cost of employee health and fringe benefits
  • Profit can stay in the company at 21% instead of hitting your personal return
  • Exists forever — ownership transfers by selling shares

✗ Things to Know

  • Double taxation — corporate tax, then personal tax on dividends
  • The most paperwork and formality of any structure
  • Board of directors, bylaws, annual meetings, written minutes required
  • Separate corporate tax return every year, whether or not you profit
  • Losses stay trapped in the company — you cannot deduct them personally
  • Higher accounting and legal costs than an LLC
  • Skipping the formalities can cost you your liability protection
The Thing Everyone Gets Wrong

Double taxation, in actual numbers.

People nod along when they hear "double taxation" without picturing what it does to real money. Say your Michigan C-Corp earns $100,000 in profit and you want to take all of it home. Here is the trip that money makes.

Step 1 — The Company Earns
$100,000

Your corporation has $100,000 of profit left after paying all its expenses, including any salary it paid you.

Step 2 — Corporate Tax
−$21,000

The corporation pays 21% federal corporate income tax on that profit. Michigan may add 6% CIT on top if the company clears the state filing threshold.

Step 3 — Dividend Tax
−$11,850

The remaining $79,000 is paid out to you as a dividend. You pay personal tax on it again — commonly 15% federal for qualified dividends, plus Michigan's 4.25%.

⚠ Roughly $67,000 of a $100,000 Profit Reaches You

The exact numbers move with your income bracket and whether the corporation owes Michigan CIT, so treat this as an illustration rather than a quote. But the shape is always the same: two separate taxes on one pile of money. An LLC or S-Corp taxed as a pass-through gets taxed once. This is the single biggest reason to think hard before incorporating.

💡 When a C-Corp Actually Makes Sense

Three situations. First, you are raising venture capital — investors require a C-Corp, usually a Delaware one, because they need preferred stock and clean cap tables. Second, you intend to reinvest profit into growth rather than pay yourself, so it sits inside the company at 21% and never gets taxed a second time. Third, you have a large employee base and want to deduct the full cost of benefits. If none of those describe you, read the Michigan LLC Guide or the Michigan S-Corp Guide first.

The Playbook

Step-by-Step: Form Your Michigan C-Corp

Ten steps from decision to open for business. Steps six and seven are the ones people skip, and skipping them is what gets liability protection thrown out in court. Do them in order.

1
Step One

Make Sure a C-Corp Is Actually What You Need

Before spending a dollar, be honest about why you are here. A lot of first-time founders reach for "Inc." because it sounds more legitimate than "LLC." It is not. Clients and banks treat both as real companies, and an LLC costs less to run and gets taxed once instead of twice.

Work through the list below. If you cannot check at least one box, a C-Corp is probably the wrong tool and you should compare it against an LLC or an S-Corp before going further.

  • You are raising or plan to raise money from venture capital or angel investors
  • You need to issue multiple classes of stock, like preferred shares for investors
  • You plan to reinvest most profits into growth rather than take them home
  • You will have foreign owners or other companies as shareholders, which an S-Corp cannot have
  • You want to offer stock options to employees as part of their pay
  • You are building toward an eventual acquisition or public offering

Your Action Step

Write down which boxes you checked and why. Then take that list to a CPA before you file anything. One conversation now costs a couple hundred dollars; converting an entity later costs far more and can trigger taxes. If you are unsure, take the business type quiz first.

⚠ Raising Venture Capital? Ask About Delaware First

Almost every venture-backed startup incorporates in Delaware, not their home state, because investors expect Delaware corporate law and standardized documents. If institutional funding is genuinely in your plan, ask your attorney about Delaware before filing in Michigan. If you are building a normal Michigan business, incorporate in Michigan — registering out of state just means paying two states instead of one.

2
Step Two

Name Your Corporation

Michigan requires a corporate name to include a word or abbreviation signaling that it is a corporation. Your options are Corporation, Incorporated, Company, or Limited, or the abbreviations Corp., Inc., Co., or Ltd. Your name also has to be distinguishable from every other entity already registered with the state.

Certain words are restricted. Anything implying you are a bank, an insurance company, or a licensed profession needs approval or proof of licensing before the state will accept it.

  • Must include Corporation, Incorporated, Company, Limited, or an abbreviation of one
  • Must be distinguishable from existing entities in the state registry
  • Cannot imply a purpose the corporation is not authorized to carry out
  • Restricted words like "bank" or "insurance" require approval
  • Check the federal trademark database before investing in branding
  • Confirm the .com domain and social media handles are free

Your Action Step

Search your name free at mibusinessregistry.lara.state.mi.us. If you are not ready to file yet but want to lock the name, file an Application for Reservation of Name (Form 540) with LARA for $10. Buy the domain the same day you settle on the name.

3
Step Three

Appoint a Resident Agent

Michigan calls this a resident agent. Most other states call it a registered agent. It is the person or company officially designated to receive legal papers and state notices for your corporation, and every Michigan corporation is required to have one.

The requirements are strict on one point: the agent needs a physical Michigan street address where someone is present during business hours. A P.O. box does not qualify. You can be your own resident agent, but that address becomes public record and you have to be reliably available during the day.

  • Must have a physical Michigan street address — no P.O. boxes
  • Must be available during normal business hours
  • Can be you, another individual, or a commercial agent service
  • The address becomes public record in the state database
  • Commercial services typically run $100–$300 per year

Your Action Step

Decide before you file, because the agent's name and address go directly on your Articles of Incorporation. If you work from home and do not want that address published, or if you travel, use a commercial resident agent service. Changing your agent later costs $5 with LARA.

⚠ Missing a Legal Notice Can Cost You the Case

If your corporation gets sued and the papers go to an agent address where nobody collects mail, the case can proceed without you and result in a default judgment. This is the whole reason the requirement exists. Whoever you name has to actually be reachable.

4
Step Four

Decide How Many Shares to Authorize

This step does not exist for LLCs and it catches almost every first-time incorporator off guard, because in Michigan the number of shares you authorize directly determines your filing fee.

Authorized shares are the maximum number of shares your corporation is permitted to issue. It is a ceiling, not a commitment. You do not have to issue them all, and most corporations issue only a fraction. Michigan charges a $10 non-refundable filing fee plus an organization fee that scales with your authorized share count.

Add the $10 filing fee to whichever row applies. A corporation authorizing 60,000 shares or fewer pays $60 total, which is the practical minimum and the right choice for the vast majority of Michigan corporations.

Your Action Step

Unless an attorney has told you otherwise for a specific funding reason, authorize 60,000 shares or fewer and pay $60. A common setup is 10,000 authorized shares with only 1,000 actually issued to the founders, leaving room to bring people in later without amending anything. If you later need more shares, you can file a Certificate of Amendment and pay the difference.

💡 Authorized Is Not the Same as Issued

Authorized shares are what you are allowed to create. Issued shares are what you have actually handed out to owners. Ownership percentage is calculated from issued shares, not authorized ones. If you authorize 10,000 and issue 1,000 split evenly between two founders, each founder owns 50% of the company, not 5%.

5
Step Five

File Your Articles of Incorporation

This is the filing that creates your corporation. In Michigan it is Form 500, Articles of Incorporation for a For-Profit Corporation, filed with LARA's Corporations Division. Your corporation legally exists the moment the state accepts it.

The form is short. You will need your corporate name, your authorized share count and their par value if any, your resident agent's name and Michigan street address, the general purpose of the business, and the name and address of each incorporator.

Your Action Step

File online at mibusinessregistry.lara.state.mi.us. Online filing is faster and cheaper to track than mailing paper. Save the endorsed, stamped copy the state sends back — your bank will want to see it and you will need it for nearly everything that follows.

6
Step Six

Adopt Corporate Bylaws

Bylaws are the rulebook for how your corporation operates. They are not filed with the state and nobody checks whether you have them, which is exactly why people skip this step. Skipping it is a mistake with real consequences.

If your corporation is ever sued, one of the first things the other side looks for is whether you actually ran it like a corporation or just used it as a shell. No bylaws, no board meetings, no minutes, personal and business money mixed together — that pattern is how a court decides to pierce the corporate veil and come after your personal assets. The liability protection you paid for evaporates.

  • Board structure — how many directors, how they are elected, how long they serve
  • Officer roles — president, secretary, treasurer, and what each is responsible for
  • Meeting rules — how often the board and shareholders meet, and how notice is given
  • Voting and quorum — how many people must be present and what margin passes a decision
  • Stock rules — how shares are issued, transferred, and valued
  • Officer authority — what size decision an officer can make alone versus needing board approval
  • Conflicts of interest — how deals involving a director or officer get handled
  • Amendment process — how the bylaws themselves get changed

Your Action Step

Draft your bylaws before your first board meeting, because the board formally adopts them at that meeting. Have a Michigan business attorney review them, especially if you have more than one shareholder. Keep the signed original in a corporate records binder along with your Articles and your stock records.

7
Step Seven

Hold Your Organizational Meeting and Issue Stock

Filing the Articles creates the legal shell. This meeting turns it into a functioning company with owners. Even if you are the only person involved, hold it, and write it down. A one-person corporation still needs a paper trail showing the corporation acted as a corporation.

Issuing stock is the part that actually makes people owners. Until shares are issued, your corporation technically has no shareholders. Each founder receives a stock certificate, and every issuance gets recorded in a stock ledger showing who owns what and what they paid for it.

  • Formally adopt the bylaws
  • Appoint the initial board of directors
  • Elect officers — president, secretary, treasurer
  • Authorize the issuance of stock to the founders and record what each paid
  • Approve opening a corporate bank account and name who can sign
  • Set the corporation's fiscal year
  • Write and sign minutes documenting every decision above

Your Action Step

Hold the meeting, take written minutes, and have every director sign them. Issue stock certificates to each founder and start a stock ledger recording the date, number of shares, and price paid. Put all of it in one corporate records binder and keep it current — this binder is your evidence that the corporation is real.

⚠ Founders: Ask a CPA About an 83(b) Election

If your founder shares vest over time rather than being owned outright from day one, there is an IRS election called an 83(b) that must be filed within 30 days of the stock being issued. The deadline is absolute and there is no fixing it late. Getting this wrong can create a large tax bill years down the road on stock you never sold. Ask before you issue, not after.

8
Step Eight

Get Your EIN (Federal Tax ID)

Every corporation is required to have an Employer Identification Number. There is no version of a C-Corp that can run on a Social Security Number, because the corporation is its own taxpayer and files its own return. You need it to open a bank account, run payroll, and file Form 1120.

  • Required for every corporation, with or without employees
  • Required to file the corporate tax return, Form 1120
  • Required to open a corporate bank account and run payroll
  • Free and instant from the IRS — about 10 minutes online
  • Apply after your Articles are approved, not before

Your Action Step

Apply free at irs.gov — Apply for EIN Online. Available Monday–Friday, 7am–10pm Eastern. An officer applies as the responsible party using their own Social Security Number. Save the confirmation letter in your corporate records binder.

⚠ Watch Out for EIN Scams

Plenty of sites charge $50–$300 to "get your EIN for you." It is unnecessary. The IRS application is free and takes about 10 minutes at irs.gov. Any site charging for an EIN is a middleman you do not need.

9
Step Nine

Open a Corporate Bank Account and Set Up Payroll

A separate bank account matters more for a corporation than for any other structure. Mixing corporate and personal money is the clearest evidence a court can find that the corporation is not really separate from you, which is precisely the argument used to strip away liability protection.

If you work in your own corporation, you are an employee of it. That means real payroll: a W-2, withheld income tax, and Social Security and Medicare contributions from both you and the company. You cannot simply move money from the corporate account to your personal one and call it pay.

  • Your endorsed Articles of Incorporation from LARA
  • Your EIN confirmation letter from the IRS
  • Your adopted corporate bylaws
  • The board resolution authorizing the account and naming who can sign
  • Government-issued photo ID for each signer
  • Your corporate records binder, in case the bank asks for anything else

Your Action Step

Open a business checking account in the corporation's exact legal name. Mercury and Relay offer free business banking suited to corporations; Huntington, Chase, and Michigan credit unions are solid traditional options. Then set up payroll through a provider like Gusto or ADP before your first paycheck. Register for Michigan withholding tax through Michigan Treasury Online and set up unemployment insurance with the Michigan UIA.

💡 Salary Is Deductible, Dividends Are Not

Reasonable salary paid to owner-employees is a deductible business expense, which lowers the corporation's taxable profit and means that money only gets taxed once, on your personal return. Dividends are paid out of profit already taxed at the corporate level. This is why C-Corp owners typically take most of their money as salary. Have a CPA help you set the split — the IRS pushes back on salaries that are unreasonably high or low.

10
Step Ten

Understand Your Tax Obligations

Your corporation files its own federal return, Form 1120, and pays a flat 21% federal tax on its taxable income. For calendar-year corporations, Form 1120 is due April 15. This return is required whether the corporation made money, lost money, or did nothing at all.

Michigan is where C-Corps get a break most people do not expect. The Michigan Corporate Income Tax is 6%, but a corporation with less than $350,000 in gross receipts apportioned to Michigan is not required to file a CIT return or pay the tax. The same exemption applies if the annual CIT liability comes to $100 or less. A large share of small Michigan corporations fall under that line entirely.

  • Federal Form 1120 — the corporate return, 21% flat rate, due April 15 for calendar-year filers
  • Michigan CIT, Form 4891 — 6%, due the last day of the fourth month after year end (April 30 for calendar-year filers)
  • CIT filing threshold — no return and no tax if Michigan gross receipts are under $350,000 or liability is $100 or less
  • Small Business Alternative Credit — an effective 1.8% rate for qualifying corporations instead of 6%
  • CIT quarterly estimates, Form 4913 — required if annual CIT liability exceeds $800; due April 15, July 15, October 15, and January 15
  • Payroll taxes — federal and Michigan withholding, plus Social Security and Medicare on every paycheck
  • Michigan sales tax — 6% if you sell taxable goods, filed through Michigan Treasury Online
  • Local city income tax — 24 Michigan cities including Detroit and Grand Rapids levy their own

💡 The Small Business Alternative Credit

Michigan offers qualifying corporations a credit that effectively drops the 6% CIT rate to 1.8% of adjusted business income. To be eligible, gross receipts must not exceed $20 million, adjusted business income must not exceed $1.3 million, and no individual officer or shareholder's allocated income can exceed $180,000. If your corporation clears the $350,000 filing threshold, ask your CPA whether you qualify — it is a meaningful difference.

⚠ File Form 1120 Even in a Year You Make Nothing

The federal corporate return is required every year the corporation exists, regardless of income. There is no "we did not do anything this year" exemption. Penalties accrue monthly on unfiled returns. If you incorporate and then set the business aside, either keep filing or formally dissolve the corporation with the state.

Your Action Step

Hire a CPA before your first fiscal year ends, not at tax time. Corporate returns are meaningfully harder than personal ones, the salary-versus-dividend split needs planning ahead of time, and the Michigan CIT thresholds and credits reward getting advice early. Register for Michigan business taxes at michigan.gov/taxes/mto.

Stay On Track

Ongoing Requirements

A corporation carries more upkeep than any other structure, and the formalities are not optional busywork. They are the evidence that keeps your liability protection intact.

Annual — $25 — May 15

Michigan Annual Report

File Form 2500 with LARA by May 15 every year. The fee is $25 if it is on time. Late fees climb month by month — $35 by the end of May, $45 in June, $55 in July, $65 in August, and $75 from September onward. Two consecutive missed years and the state can dissolve your corporation.

Annual — April 15

Federal Form 1120

The corporate income tax return, taxed at a flat 21%. Due April 15 for calendar-year corporations. Required every single year the corporation exists, profitable or not.

Annual — April 30

Michigan CIT Return

File Form 4891 by the last day of the fourth month after your tax year ends. Skip it only if your Michigan gross receipts are under $350,000 or your liability is $100 or less. Quarterly estimates on Form 4913 are required once liability tops $800.

Annual

Board and Shareholder Meetings

Hold them, and write minutes. This is the formality that most often decides whether a court respects your corporate shield. Even a single-shareholder corporation should document its annual meeting in writing every year.

Every Pay Period

Payroll and Withholding

Owner-employees get W-2 paychecks with proper withholding. File federal Form 941 quarterly, Michigan withholding through MTO, and W-2s by January 31. A payroll provider handles this automatically and is worth the cost.

Ongoing

Corporate Records and Stock Ledger

Keep the Articles, bylaws, all meeting minutes, resolutions, and the stock ledger together and current. Update the ledger every time shares are issued or transferred. Never pay personal expenses from the corporate account.

You're Ready

That's your complete Michigan C-Corp playbook.

File the Articles, adopt the bylaws, issue the stock, and keep the records. Ready to dig into the tax side or compare this against a simpler structure?
